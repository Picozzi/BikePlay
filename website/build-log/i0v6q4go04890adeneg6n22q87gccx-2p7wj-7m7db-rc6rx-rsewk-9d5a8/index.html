Build Log - Week 7

March 3, 2023

BikePlay is done! (for demo at least)

All of the 3D-prints were completed and collected from WatIMake early this week and all assembled properly. See below for photos of the assembled controller module!

Two new visors have also been laser so that a user can wear the helmet in two different lighting conditions: sunny and overcast. To do this, two polarized sheets will be applied to each visor: one at 0° relative to the visor and another at 80° or 30° for sunny and overcast, respectively. This will correctly filter the amount of light passing through the visor so that the user can still properly see both the projected visuals and their surroundings.

With the completion of all the housings, the electrical components have also all been mounted in their proper housing, which have been attached to the helmet. All components have been properly wired together and tested, with the last step being to insulate the wires using heat shrink.

Another exciting update is in the firmware for BikePlay. All information can be properly sent from the app and properly received and interpreted by the RPi! Most visuals for the projections on the visor have been created, including arrows for direction and mapping for text and numbers for the time, directions, temperature, and battery level. The last task in firmware is to finish creating a few of the weather icons, which will be completed by the end of this weekend.

All pages of the app are also now complete and look great! Take a look below.

With the helmet now fully assembled, testing is underway with several different users to ensure the system works for multiple people.

One problem that we encountered last weekend was that the field of view of users is slightly smaller than anticipated. Even though users could see the full height of the projection, they could only see ⅔ of the width. This was due to two factors: first, the sides of the lens bezel blocked some of the image. Thankfully, reprinting the lens bezel with a thinner border eliminated this problem so users can now see ¾ of the width. The second problem is that each projected image is located in a spot where the user can only read it with one eye. As such, their field of view is reduced. Due to mechanical constraints, we are unable to move the image farther to the centre of the visor, where they would be able to see it with both eyes. Our solution was therefore to compress the visuals a bit so that they all fit within the middle ¾ of the screen and can therefore be seen by the user.
